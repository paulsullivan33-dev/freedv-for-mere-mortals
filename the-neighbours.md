# The Neighbours

FreeDV sits among several other ways of
getting a voice — or the *content* of
one — across the air. This chapter says
what each neighbour does that FreeDV
does not, and the reverse, so you can
choose deliberately. The neighbours are
characterised from their documentation
and general practice, not benchmarked;
the lab ran none of them.

## Plain SSB

The incumbent. SSB needs no computer, no
codec, and no sync; it fades gracefully,
so a skilled ear can mine a
half-destroyed sentence for the word
that mattered. FreeDV's counter-claim is
narrow and real: on paths past SSB's
cliff — and chapter 7 shows the shape of
that cliff for one laboratory channel —
the digital voice is still whole, and
it asks for less bandwidth and, usually,
less power. What SSB keeps forever: zero
delay, zero setup, and a signal any
listener can identify by ear.

## The VHF/UHF digital voice modes

DMR, D-Star and System Fusion are
FreeDV's cousins by another mother:
digital voice, thriving on VHF and UHF
through repeaters and hotspots, with
talkgroups and networks instead of
ionospheric luck. They are more
polished, more populated, and — the
difference this series cares about —
built on a **closed, patented codec**
(AMBE). You can buy a radio that speaks
it; you cannot build the codec from
published sources, repair it, or run it
in a file pipeline like chapters 5 to 7.
FreeDV is what you get when the same
idea is built the open way, for HF.

## FT8 and the weak-signal data modes

WSJT-X's modes (this series has a
WSJT-X guide) go far beyond FreeDV in
weak-signal reach — they decode signals
far below the noise a voice could ever
survive. The price is that there is no
voice: a contact is a handful of
structured exchanges, call signs, a
grid, a report. If the point is *a
conversation*, FreeDV is the digital
mode that still has one. If the point
is *the contact at any cost*, FT8 wins
and it is not close.

## The sound-card data modes

Fldigi, Dire Wolf and their kin (guides
in this series cover both) move *text
and files* over the same audio path
FreeDV uses, by keyboard rather than by
voice. They share FreeDV's plumbing —
sound cards, levels, PTT — which is why
an operator set up for one is nearly
set up for all. Choose them when the
message is the message; choose FreeDV
when the message is a person talking,
with the pauses, emphasis and identity
that text throws away.

## The one-line version

SSB for simplicity, the closed modes
for repeater polish, FT8 for the
impossible contact, the data modes for
text — and FreeDV when you want a human
voice, on HF, from weak paths, through
a stack you are allowed to read.
